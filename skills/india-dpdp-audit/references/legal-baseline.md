# Legal starting points

Last verified: 2026-10-27 (Gazette texts, the PIB releases and every link below checked on that date). Review cadence: re-verify before each regulatory date (Consent Manager phase and full enforcement). 13 November 2026 and 13 May 2027 are conservative working preparation dates derived from the printed Gazette date (13 November 2025); the legal trigger is the period from publication, and the exact calendar day is unresolved until verified and on any MeitY notice. Reverify at every audit; this is a source map for audit work, not legal advice or a legal opinion.

## Primary sources

- [DPDP Act 2023, official copy](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf): sections 3–7 scope/grounds/notice/consent, 8 fiduciary duties, 9 children/guardians, 10 SDF, 11–14 rights, 16 transfers, 17 exemptions. The original Act alone does not establish commencement.
- [G.S.R. 843(E), commencement](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf), Gazette 13 November 2025: immediate institutional provisions; sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) after one year; most substantive processing obligations after eighteen months. Section 44(2), relevant to IT Act amendments, is in the eighteen-month group.
- [Final DPDP Rules 2025, G.S.R. 846(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf), Gazette 13 November 2025. English begins PDF page 24. Rule 1 phases commencement: rules 1, 2, 17–21 immediately; rule 4 after one year; rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23 after eighteen months. Consult the exact rule text and schedules for conditional thresholds and exceptions.
- [G.S.R. 892(E), corrigenda](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/3c7ebbae0e5456f493f486e6845df86b.pdf), dated 10 December 2025, Gazette 11 December 2025: includes correction of commencement wording to publication “in the Official Gazette.” Apply corrections when reading the Rules.
- [PIB, 16 May 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2261823): PIB reports the MeitY Secretary, at an 11 May 2026 workshop, referring to the DPDP Act "becoming fully enforceable from 13 May 2027". Use this as corroboration of the Gazette timeline, not as a substitute for notification text. Some government website summaries use 14 November 2025 as the notification/upload date; retain that distinction.
- [SPDI Rules 2011, G.S.R. 313(E), government-hosted Gazette](https://emb.mes.gov.in/Common/help/gsr3_10511.pdf) and [PIB press note (MeitY/DIT clarification), 24 August 2011](https://pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=74990) (official PIB text; archived MeitY PDF: [Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20241118102156/https://www.meity.gov.in/writereaddata/files/PressNote_25811.pdf)): assess present applicability separately, including Rule 4 privacy policy and the clarification on contractual processing and Rules 5/6. Do not treat all ordinary contact information as SPDI or assume the absence of SPDI resolves every Rule 4 question. The SPDI Rules rest on IT Act section 43A, which DPDP section 44(2) omits when the eighteen-month group commences.

At the last verification, most public-facing DPDP requirements are **readiness requirements**, with the substantive phase due May 2027. Rule 4 (Consent Manager registration) and sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) commence one year after publication, i.e. 13 November 2026 (some sources compute 14 November); the other Consent Manager provisions (s.6(7)-(8)) sit in the eighteen-month group. Confirm exact dates and subsequent changes before stating a deadline in a report.

**Warning (unverified):** secondary reporting says MeitY consulted in January-February 2026 on shortening the eighteen-month period to twelve months; no Gazette notification of that change was found at the last verification, and the MeitY listing pages could not be searched (they render by JavaScript). This is unverified secondary reporting, not established fact. Check the e-Gazette for any amendment to G.S.R. 843(E) or Rule 1, and re-check the whole calendar against any Gazette notice, before relying on May 2027.

## October 2026 Removal of Difficulties Order

[Official S.O. 5458(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/10/daf495a7b5798a3dfa72a944b51fc176.pdf), order dated 5 October 2026, Gazette issue dated 6 October, digitally published 7 October: verified English text on PDF pages 2–3. It clarifies section 9(1)'s parallel reference to a child or a person with disability who has a lawful guardian, and changes section 10(2)(c)(ii) to periodic data audit. It contains no change to the substantive commencement timetable. Apply these corrections rather than treating the supplied 2023 Act PDF as consolidated current law.

## Interpretation checks

- Section 5/Rule 3 consent notice is purpose-specific; a generic policy may not be enough. Assess the actual lawful ground before finding a consent-notice violation.
- Section 8(9) permits an appropriate authorised contact where a statutory DPO is not required. Section 10 additional duties depend on SDF designation.
- Sections 11–14 and Rule 14 cover distinct rights; do not substitute a generic “contact us” promise for an operational route without checking usability.
- Rule 6 safeguards and Rule 7 breach notifications need internal evidence. An HTTPS padlock proves neither.
- Rule 8 and schedules require careful retention analysis; avoid applying category-specific inactivity periods to every site or treating one-year security retention as permission for indefinite general retention.
- Section 9 and Rules 10–12 require applicability and exemption checks. General business positioning is not proof that no children use a service.
- Transfer restrictions require the actual current notifications and sector rules. DPDP section 16 restricts only countries the government notifies, not foreign hosting in general; check Rule 13(4) for SDFs and sector rules (e.g. RBI payment-data storage).
- GDPR/ePrivacy requirements, cookie-banner conventions and general security hardening are not automatically Indian statutory requirements. Label any separately applicable law or optional advice.
